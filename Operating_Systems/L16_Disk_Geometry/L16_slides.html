🎪 Record Needles02 / 04

Mechanical HDD vs. Silicon SSD Analogies

Hard Disk Drive (HDD): Like a classic vinyl record player. Moving the arm head to the track (Seek Time) and spinning the disk to the sector (Rotational Latency) takes real, slow physical time.

Solid-State Drive (SSD): Like a massive grid of millions of light switches on a wall. There are no moving parts, reading states instantly. However, cells wear out over time, demanding an active Wear Leveling rotation scheme.

🔍 Asymmetric NAND03 / 04

SSD Architecture & Flash Constraints

Silicon storage introduces an asymmetric structural barrier:

• Data reads and writes operate on a fine, high-speed Page level layout footprint.

• Data deletion can only be triggered in massive, coarse Block tiers simultaneously.

• The internal **Flash Translation Layer (FTL)** dynamically remaps block sectors to evenly distribute erasure fatigue across cells.

🛠️ HDD Access Math04 / 04

Calculating Mechanical Data Latencies

Evaluating performance under a 7200 RPM, 4ms Seek Time, and 100MB/s Transfer Rate setup for a single 512-byte sector block:

1. Rotational Latency: 7200 RPM = 120 rot/sec -> Full Rotation = 8.33ms. On average, wait for half a rotation = 8.33ms / 2 = 4.17ms. 2. Transfer Duration: 512 bytes / 100,000,000 bytes/sec = 0.005ms. 3. Sum Access Cost: 4.000ms (Seek) + 4.170ms (Rotation) + 0.005ms (Transfer) Total Access Latency = 8.175ms (99.9% spent fighting mechanical inertia!)
